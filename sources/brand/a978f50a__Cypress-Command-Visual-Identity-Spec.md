# Cypress Command Visual Identity Specification

**Version 1.0 · Implementation standard · 20 September 2026**

> **Purpose.** Cypress Command is a modern operating-systems and AI advisory brand for real businesses. Its identity makes complicated work feel clear, ordered, and practical. The system combines a sober editorial voice with the discipline of a technical manual: **people, systems, real work, and a more capable tomorrow.**

This specification translates the approved Cypress Command reference material into implementable standards. It is a standard for designers, developers, printers, and production partners; it does not create or replace the approved mark artwork.

> **Legal and ownership notice.** The final mark’s ownership, registrability, and trademark clearance are **unverified**. Do not represent the approved squared spiral/C mark as registered, exclusive, cleared, or owned until counsel has confirmed those matters. Use the approved artwork only under the project’s existing authorization, and complete a trademark search and chain-of-title review before public launch or filing.

## 1. Brand character and operating principle

Cypress Command should feel **capable rather than futuristic, grounded rather than rustic, and exact rather than cold**. The visual language is rooted in operational reality: property, work sites, storefronts, teams, documents, systems, and outcomes. It uses paper-like space, black editorial type, restrained rules, and a small set of semantic inks to make dense information easy to enter.

Every layout should answer three questions in this order: **What is the operating outcome? What is the system that produces it? What is the next practical action?** The system is deliberately quiet around the message. Hierarchy, spacing, and factual specificity do more work than decoration.

The identity has four compatible expressions. They share the same mark, type hierarchy, spacing discipline, and palette; a project may emphasize one expression without inventing a separate style.

| Expression | Primary job | Visual emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| **Editorial** | Brand, web, proposals, articles | Generous Paper space, Libertinus display type, confident headlines, measured imagery |
| **Technical** | Operating diagrams, SOPs, systems | Modules, step numbers, rules, direct arrows, labeled inputs and outcomes |
| **Archival** | Thought leadership, research, reports | Figure numbers, captions, quiet plates, quotations, evidence-led tables |
| **Digital Command** | Product UI, dashboards, portals | Inter-led interface type, compact cards, clear statuses, practical navigation |

## 2. Logo family

### 2.1 The mark

The emblem is the approved **squared spiral/C mark taken from the uploaded brand pages**: one continuous outer C-shaped loop with squared internal geometry and a detached rectangular center terminal. It is a symbol of command, connection, and an operating loop. It must be supplied from the approved extracted master vector; it must **not** be redrawn, substituted with a different C construction, stretched, outlined, rounded, or rebuilt from a type character.

For measurement below, **x** equals the width of the emblem’s primary structural stroke (the recurring bar thickness within the approved mark). The emblem’s bounding box is square. Measure from the visible edge of the filled mark, not from an SVG artboard or invisible bounding box.

### 2.2 Approved configurations

| Configuration | Construction | Primary use | Minimum digital size | Minimum print size |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| **Primary horizontal lockup** | Emblem left; `CYPRESS` over `COMMAND` in Inter, uppercase, at the approved relationship | Website header, proposal/report cover, slides, stationery | 120 px wide | 30 mm wide |
| **Compact horizontal lockup** | Emblem left; Cypress Command on one line, only from approved master | Tight headers, email/footer, small sponsorship placements | 104 px wide | 26 mm wide |
| **Stacked lockup** | Emblem above the two-line wordmark | Narrow cover, vertical format, social header | 88 px wide | 22 mm wide |
| **Emblem only** | Standalone squared spiral/C | Favicon, app icon, avatar, UI product switcher, small spaces | 16 px; prefer 20 px+ | 5 mm; prefer 7 mm+ |
| **Endorsement line** | Approved lockup followed by `by Cypress Command` in Inter | Playbook, partner or sub-brand attribution | 130 px wide | 33 mm wide |

Use the primary horizontal lockup whenever space permits. The emblem-only version is a recognition device, not a substitute for the full name on first exposure. On an unfamiliar page, introduce the full lockup in the header, footer, or first meaningful branded panel.

### 2.3 Colorways

The preferred identity colorway is a **Terra spiral/C mark with an Ink wordmark on Paper**. On an Ink or photographic dark field, use the Paper mark and wordmark, retaining Terra only where it is part of an approved accent treatment. A single-color version may be Ink on Paper, Paper on Ink, or Paper on Olive, Terra, or Oxblood when contrast requirements are met.

Do not apply gradients, transparency, shadows, textures, photo masks, outlines, patterns, or multi-color fragments to the mark. Do not recolor it Mustard for a primary identifier. Mustard is a progression cue, not a corporate signature.

### 2.4 Clear space, placement, and scale

Keep a minimum clear space of **2x** around every logo configuration. Clear space is measured from the outermost visible edge of the emblem or wordmark, whichever extends furthest. No type, rule, image edge, container edge, partner mark, or UI control may enter this zone. In exceptionally crowded interface chrome, emblem-only clear space may reduce to **1x**, but never below it.

Align the lockup to the page grid, not optically to unrelated image features. Keep it out of the lower-right corner of photography, where it competes with captions and calls to action. On a report cover, the logo may sit in the top-left grid column; on a proposal, a Terra vertical spine may carry the mark only when there is 2x clear space on every side.

Never reproduce the full lockup below its minimum size. When the minimum cannot be met, use the emblem only and provide the full name in nearby live text. Maintain the supplied aspect ratio in every output. A logo must never become wider, taller, heavier, lighter, letterspaced, or center-aligned differently from the approved master.

### 2.5 Incorrect use

**Do** use the approved vector master, one approved colorway, sufficient clear space, and a clear contrast field. **Do not** rotate the mark, add a keyline, crop it, place it over busy image detail, substitute a generic letter C, rebuild it from rounded strokes, or set the wordmark in Libertinus Serif. Do not make the emblem decorative wallpaper or repeat it as a pattern. Do not imply trademark registration with ® or ™ until legal direction authorizes it.

## 3. Core color system

The palette is warm, restrained, and semantic. **Paper and Ink establish the environment; the four color inks communicate state or emphasis.** Color must clarify meaning, not decorate empty space. The following hexadecimal values are the digital source of truth.

| Token | Hex | Role | Typical use |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Paper** | `#F4EFE2` | Primary light surface | Page canvas, open space, primary light panels |
| **Paper Deep** | `#E8E2CD` | Secondary light surface | Cards, sidebars, pull quotes, recessed panels |
| **Ink** | `#1E1B16` | Primary dark ink | Headlines, body text, icons, dark canvas, strong rules |
| **Ink Soft** | `#5C554A` | Secondary ink | Metadata, captions, supporting labels, inactive text where contrast passes |
| **Rule** | `#C9BFA8` | Structural neutral | Dividers, table lines, subtle borders, inactive structure |
| **Olive** | `#49573C` | Success / stable system | Complete, positive movement, durable outcomes |
| **Mustard** | `#8F6D0C` | Progress / attention | In progress, active stage, controlled attention |
| **Terra** | `#A44E12` | Primary action / editorial accent | Primary action, section spine, selected state, key chapter cue |
| **Oxblood** | `#8A2F1F` | Risk / critical state | Errors, blockers, high-risk decisions, critical calls to action |

### 3.1 Usage proportions and semantic rules

In a typical light editorial page, Paper should occupy roughly **70–85%** of visible area, Ink and Ink Soft should provide the reading structure, and Paper Deep should define contained regions. Reserve colored inks for a small, purposeful share of the page. Terra is the only default action color. Olive, Mustard, and Oxblood are status colors, so they require a matching status word, icon, or label; color alone is never the message.

A page needs no more than one dominant semantic accent in a given local composition. For example, a four-stage operating flow can use Olive, Mustard, Terra, and Oxblood as stage rails, but its body copy, nodes, and rules remain neutral. Do not use the four accents as arbitrary category colors in every chart, deck, or interface.

### 3.2 Accessibility pairings

Contrast ratios below are calculated from the approved hex values under WCAG relative-luminance math. A normal-text pairing needs at least **4.5:1**. Large text—at least 24 px regular or 18.66 px bold in web terms—needs at least **3:1**. Non-text UI boundaries and graphics need at least **3:1** against their adjacent color. Verify the final rendered state when opacity, photography, anti-aliasing, or an ICC profile changes the appearance.

| Foreground on background | Ratio | Permitted use |
|---|---:|---|
| Ink on Paper | 14.95:1 | All text, icons, rules, and essential UI |
| Ink on Paper Deep | 13.23:1 | All text, icons, and essential UI |
| Ink Soft on Paper | 6.41:1 | Body-supporting text, metadata, captions, icons |
| Paper on Olive | 6.75:1 | All text and icons on stable/success controls |
| Paper on Terra | 4.97:1 | All text and icons on primary action controls |
| Paper on Oxblood | 7.30:1 | All text and icons on critical controls |
| Paper Deep on Olive | 5.97:1 | All text and icons where Paper Deep is deliberately used |
| Paper Deep on Oxblood | 6.46:1 | All text and icons where Paper Deep is deliberately used |
| Paper on Mustard | 4.19:1 | Large/bold text and non-text UI only; not normal-size text |
| Ink on Mustard | 3.56:1 | Large/bold text and non-text UI only; not normal-size text |
| Paper Deep on Terra | 4.40:1 | Large/bold text only; use Paper for normal text |

Mustard is therefore best used as a rail, indicator, underline, chart segment with direct label, or large-number stage marker. Do not set paragraph text, small button labels, form labels, or thin icons in either Paper or Ink on Mustard. Never use Ink on Olive or Ink on Terra for required text. Where a semantic status appears, include a written label such as **Complete**, **In progress**, **Needs review**, or **Blocked** and do not rely on hue alone.

For focus indication, use a 2 px Ink focus ring on Paper interfaces and a 2 px Paper focus ring on Ink interfaces, offset by 2 px. The focus ring must remain visible without hover or color perception.

## 4. Typography

The typography system pairs an editorial serif with an efficient humanist sans. The serif gives Cypress Command a considered point of view; the sans makes operational information quick to scan.

| Role | Preferred family | Practical stack | Approved use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Editorial display and headings** | Libertinus Serif | `"Libertinus Serif", Georgia, "Times New Roman", serif` | Display statements, article/report headings, quotations, key numbers in editorial work |
| **Interface, body, labels, navigation** | Inter | `Inter, Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif` | Body copy, UI, buttons, diagrams, tables, metadata, wordmark support |
| **Fallback when web fonts cannot load** | Arial | `Arial, Helvetica, sans-serif` | Digital fallback for all sans-led applications; use a restrained serif system fallback for display |

Use the current, legally permitted releases of **Libertinus Serif** and **Inter**, both practical open-license choices. Package approved font files or load them from the organization’s approved font delivery method; do not assume a visitor has either family installed. If font loading fails, preserve hierarchy through size, weight, and spacing rather than attempting a decorative replacement. Arial is an acceptable functional fallback for Inter-led environments, especially enterprise software and generated documents.

Libertinus Serif should appear at Regular, Semibold, or Bold according to the supplied files; use its italic for quotations or restrained emphasis. Inter should use Regular (400), Medium (500), Semibold (600), and Bold (700). Avoid very light weights. Do not use more than three weights in one view. Use tabular numerals in Inter for dashboards, tables, financial figures, and process timing.

### 4.1 Type scale

The scale uses an 8 px rhythm and allows small adjustments for the exact medium. The sizes below are web CSS pixels; print equivalents should preserve the stated hierarchy rather than forcing a direct unit conversion.

| Style | Family / weight | Desktop size / line-height | Mobile size / line-height | Tracking and case | Use |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Display | Libertinus Serif Bold | 64 / 64 | 44 / 48 | -0.025em, sentence case | Hero, cover, major thesis |
| H1 | Libertinus Serif Semibold | 48 / 52 | 36 / 40 | -0.02em, sentence case | Page title |
| H2 | Libertinus Serif Semibold | 32 / 38 | 28 / 34 | -0.015em, sentence case | Major section |
| H3 | Inter Semibold | 24 / 30 | 22 / 28 | -0.01em, sentence case | Module, card, sub-section |
| H4 | Inter Semibold | 18 / 24 | 18 / 24 | 0, sentence case | Small section, component title |
| Body | Inter Regular | 16 / 26 | 16 / 25 | 0, sentence case | Primary reading text |
| Body small | Inter Regular | 14 / 21 | 14 / 20 | 0, sentence case | Dense cards, support copy |
| Caption / metadata | Inter Medium | 12 / 16 | 12 / 16 | 0.02em, sentence case | Figure caption, date, byline |
| Eyebrow / label | Inter Semibold | 11 / 14 | 11 / 14 | 0.12em, uppercase | Section marker, category, status label |
| Micro label | Inter Semibold | 10 / 12 | 10 / 12 | 0.10em, uppercase | Only controlled UI or print annotation |

Limit display text to approximately 8–12 words and no more than three lines in a hero. Keep editorial body measures between **45 and 75 characters**. Do not set full paragraphs in all caps, justified alignment, or tightly tracked serif text. Inter labels may be uppercase when short; use letter spacing rather than increasing font weight to create a technical register.

### 4.2 Type hierarchy in practice

A canonical page begins with an Inter eyebrow, a Libertinus title, and an Inter deck or summary. Section markers use a two-digit Inter number such as `01`, a 1 px Rule, and a concise label. Figures use `Fig. 01` in Inter Semibold and a clear descriptive caption in Inter Regular. Quote blocks use Libertinus Serif italic or regular, not oversized quotation marks.

## 5. Layout, grid, and spacing

### 5.1 Layout grid

Use a modular grid with visible but not intrusive discipline. Content aligns to columns, rules, and repeated card edges. The grid is a tool for comparison and reading—not a decorative overlay.

| Breakpoint / medium | Grid | Outer margin | Gutter | Recommended content behavior |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Desktop, 1440 px and above | 12 columns | 64 px | 24 px | Full editorial and technical compositions; hero may use 7/5 or 6/6 split |
| Desktop, 1024–1439 px | 12 columns | 48 px | 20 px | Maintain full hierarchy; convert secondary rail when needed |
| Tablet, 768–1023 px | 8 columns | 32 px | 16 px | Preserve 2–4 column card groups; move sidebars beneath content |
| Mobile, 320–767 px | 4 columns | 20 px | 12 px | Single-column reading; two compact cards only when content remains legible |
| Print letter/A4 | 12 columns | 15 mm minimum | 4 mm | Use a 2–3 column editorial body or 12-column technical plate; retain 6 mm internal card padding minimum |

Use a **4 px micro-unit** and an **8 px base rhythm**. Approved spacing tokens are: `4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128 px`. Apply 24–32 px between related modules, 48–64 px between major sections, and 96 px only for major editorial transitions. Use 16–24 px internal padding on digital cards and 24–32 px on feature cards; do not make every container equally padded.

### 5.2 Rules, frames, and surfaces

Rules create the technical-manual character. Use a **1 px Rule** line for ordinary dividers, table rows, card edges, and section baselines. Use a **2 px Ink** line only for a major structural boundary or a focused/selected state. Use a **2–4 px semantic color rail** at the top or left of a stage, card, chapter, or alert; it is a state cue, not a replacement for a label. Do not use rounded pill outlines as a primary layout motif.

Cards are primarily Paper or Paper Deep with a 1 px Rule border. Corners should be square or minimally softened at **2–4 px** in digital product UI only. Editorial and technical plates default to square corners. Shadows are generally prohibited; establish depth using Paper/Paper Deep contrast, a rule, and spacing. If a floating digital surface genuinely requires elevation, use one restrained low-opacity Ink shadow and document it in the component library; do not use it on every card.

## 6. Graphic language: icons, imagery, diagrams, and data

### 6.1 Iconography

Icons are clear, work-oriented, and structurally simple. Draw on a **24 × 24 px grid** with a 1.5 px stroke, round line caps and joins only where needed for legibility, and predominantly squared geometry. At 16 px, use a tested simplified icon and a 1.5–2 px effective stroke rather than merely scaling down a complex drawing. Use Ink as the default icon color; use a semantic ink only when it reinforces an adjacent written status.

Prefer literal operational symbols—property, document, people, work order, tools, calendar, chart, location, settings, feedback, clock—over abstract AI sparkles, robots, brains, or novelty glyphs. Icons should be outline-led with limited solid areas. An icon should not contain more than one semantic color, and a visible text label is required for unfamiliar or consequential controls.

### 6.2 Photography and image treatment

Choose **real places, real people, and real progress**. Suitable subjects include operating teams at work, regional retail or property environments, streetscapes, shop fronts, worksites, material details, planning sessions, records, and built environments. Images should feel candid, orderly, and specific to a business context. Favor natural or warm neutral light, practical viewpoints, tactile materials, and compositions with enough quiet area for a headline or caption.

Use a restrained, slightly muted grade that preserves believable skin tones and detail. Photography may sit in a clean rectangular crop, a ruled plate, or a full-bleed dark presentation frame. If type overlays photography, first create a stable contrast field with a tested Ink overlay or a Paper panel; never depend on an untested busy image. Avoid generic server racks, glowing blue AI visuals, robots, holograms, synthetic faces, gratuitous city-night neon, heavily staged handshake stock, and overly polished lifestyle imagery.

### 6.3 Illustrations and diagrams

Diagrams make operating systems tangible. They should be clean, functional, editorial, and human-led. Use Ink line work on Paper, measured annotation, small Inter labels, and simple rectangular modules. A diagram should state its point in the title, define inputs and outputs, show a clear reading direction, and end with an observable outcome. Use arrows sparingly and consistently; for a linear flow, move left to right. Use a return path beneath the flow for iteration or continuous improvement.

Technical diagrams should typically use this structure: **title and figure label; inputs; numbered system stages; outputs; principles or notes.** Each stage has an explicit verb, owner or mode where useful, and short supporting text. Stage numbers may be large and colored, but stage titles and explanations remain Ink. Use Olive for complete/stable, Mustard for in-progress, Terra for active/action, and Oxblood for risk/blocked only when the state is real.

Use 1 px Rule for internal dividers, 1–1.5 px Ink for directional arrows, and 2–4 px semantic rails for state. Keep diagram text at or above 12 px on screen and 7.5 pt in print. Do not use isometric illustrations, glossy 3D diagrams, ambiguous loop arrows, rainbow flows, or unlabeled icons. A diagram must remain comprehensible when reproduced in grayscale; labels, numbering, line style, and position carry the primary meaning.

### 6.4 Data visualization

Data views should read like evidence, not decoration. Use Paper or Ink canvas; Ink for primary series and type; Rule for grid lines; and semantic inks only for an explained highlight or status. Lead with a precise chart title that states the finding, then include a time frame, unit, and source/definition where applicable. Prefer direct data labels for small series and contextual annotation for important inflections.

| Data condition | Default visual treatment |
|---|---|
| Baseline / total | Ink bar, line, or label |
| Stable / achieved / positive | Olive with a written positive-state label |
| In progress / target range | Mustard with label or patterned support |
| Active action / selected comparison | Terra with label and selected-state treatment |
| Error / risk / below threshold | Oxblood with label and explicit threshold or explanation |

Use horizontal bar charts for ranked categories, line charts for time, and simple tables when precise lookup matters. Start bar charts at zero unless a nonzero baseline is disclosed prominently. Avoid pie charts with more than five segments, 3D effects, gradients, dual axes without a compelling reason, ornamental gauges, and color-only legends. In dark mode, keep Paper labels and direct annotations high contrast; do not make a chart reliant on faint Rule lines.

## 7. Dark mode

Dark mode is a true companion expression, not an inverted screenshot. It should feel like a quiet operations room: warm, legible, and low-noise.

| Role | Dark-mode application |
|---|---|
| Canvas | Ink `#1E1B16` |
| Primary text and key icon | Paper `#F4EFE2` |
| Secondary text | Paper Deep `#E8E2CD`; do not use Ink Soft for required dark-mode text |
| Recessed surface | Paper Deep at 6–12% opacity over Ink; test final compositing contrast |
| Borders / table rules | Rule at a restrained opacity, increased as needed to meet 3:1 for essential boundaries |
| Primary action | Terra with Paper label/icon |
| Success / stable | Olive with Paper label/icon |
| Critical | Oxblood with Paper label/icon |
| Progress | Mustard as rail, indicator, or large text only; include label |

On dark surfaces, use Paper for high-priority content and Paper Deep for support content. Keep cards dark and modestly differentiated rather than switching every card to Paper. The primary logo treatment is Paper and Terra on Ink. Do not invert semantic meanings, turn all surfaces pure black, or introduce electric blues, bright whites, glass effects, or neon gradients.

Offer the same information, controls, focus treatment, status labels, and state distinctions in each mode. Do not use dark mode solely to hide low-contrast UI. Test dark layouts in a bright environment and at reduced brightness; adjust opacity and border treatment in the component library rather than inventing new ad hoc colors.

## 8. Core layout patterns

### 8.1 Website hero

Use a header with the primary horizontal lockup, focused navigation, and one Terra primary action. The hero uses a 7/5 or 6/6 grid: a Libertinus Display proposition on the left, an authentic contextual image or operational plate on the right. Below the proposition, use an Inter body deck and no more than two actions. A four-step operational strip—**Strategize, Design, Install, Operate**—may sit along the bottom edge using numbered items and Rule dividers. The objective is calm orientation, not a dense product landing page.

### 8.2 Proposal or report cover

Place the logo in the upper-left, proposal status and date in compact Inter metadata, and a specific operating-system title in Libertinus Serif. Use a Terra spine or top rail as the only strong accent. A documentary image may occupy the lower third or a defined panel. Place client and Cypress Command credits in a ruled footer. Avoid huge logos, generic abstract AI art, and multiple competing accent colors.

### 8.3 Thought-leadership article

Begin with a small eyebrow, a substantial editorial headline, a concise deck, author/date/read-time metadata, and one relevant image or figure. Use a narrow reading measure with a right-side contents rail at larger breakpoints. Figures are enclosed in subtle Rule frames with `Fig. 01` captions. Body pages should give breathing room around quotes, diagrams, and tables while retaining a clear baseline rhythm.

### 8.4 Technical operating flow

Open with a plain-language title and a one-line outcome. Then show stages as numbered modules with a colored top rail and neutral bodies. Place **inputs** at the left, **outputs** at the right, and an iteration line below the main flow. Supporting enablers—people, data, tools, processes—sit as a second row, separated from the primary flow. Make ownership, review points, and risk visible; do not let an abstract process diagram imply that AI operates without human judgment.

### 8.5 Digital Command dashboard

Use the emblem or compact lockup in a Paper Deep sidebar or Ink top bar. Navigation is Inter, icon-supported, and label-first. Present a calm greeting, a small set of consequential metrics, an evidence-led chart, and an activity list. Cards must have direct titles, units, status text, and enough white space for reading. Terra denotes the primary action or selected state; Olive, Mustard, and Oxblood remain semantic. Avoid decorative gradients, excessive rounded cards, tiny dashboard type, or status colors applied without written meaning.

### 8.6 Presentation slide

Treat each slide as a page of an operating narrative. Use one thought per slide, a Libertinus title, a clear image/diagram or concise evidence block, and an Inter footer. Dark presentation covers can use Ink with Paper type, a Terra rule, and a contextual image; content slides may return to Paper. Use slide numbers and short topic labels consistently. Never reduce body text below a comfortable presentation size simply to preserve a layout.

## 9. Components and interactive behavior

Primary actions use a Terra fill with Paper label and a directional arrow only where it represents a real next step. Default height is **44 px** or greater, horizontal padding is 16–20 px, and label text is Inter Semibold 14/20. Secondary actions use Paper with a 1 px Ink border and Ink text. Tertiary actions are text links in Ink or Terra with an underline or arrow; they must not depend on color alone.

Inputs have a Paper surface, 1 px Rule border, 44 px minimum height, Inter 14/20 text, always-visible label, and clear error/help text. Checkboxes and toggles need visible checked/unchecked states that remain distinguishable without color. Status chips should be compact rectangular labels with a color dot plus written state, never a color-only pill.

All interactive targets must be at least **44 × 44 px** unless a documented dense-data exception provides equivalent alternative access. Support keyboard navigation, a visible focus ring, reduced motion preferences, text resizing to 200%, and error messages that explain the problem and recovery action. Motion should be short, functional, and optional: 150–200 ms for ordinary state changes, with no decorative parallax or looping animation.

## 10. Print and digital production

### 10.1 Print

Use the supplied master vectors for the mark and keep typography live until final proof where the printer permits. For editorial documents, prefer uncoated or lightly textured warm-white stock that supports the Paper character; do not specify the brand Paper hex as a literal paper-stock color. Request physical proofs for covers, Terra spines, dark Ink fields, and any process with large solid color areas.

Build print files at final trim size with **3 mm / 0.125 in bleed**, printer-specified slug, crop marks only when requested, embedded or outlined approved fonts as required by the printer, and vector rules/icons. Use at least **0.25 pt** rules in print; use 0.5 pt where a rule must survive ordinary office reproduction. Keep reverse type at 7 pt or above in a robust weight; use 8 pt or above for ordinary body copy. Avoid fine reversed serif text and very thin lines over image or color fields.

Hex values are the brand’s digital source of truth. Convert to the printer’s requested ICC profile only at final production, retain the original named swatches, and approve a contract or calibrated proof. Do not assign unofficial CMYK, spot, or Pantone equivalents as permanent standards without proofing under the intended stock, ink, and press conditions. For one-color documents, reproduce the system in Ink and use line style, labels, and hierarchy—not converted accent colors—to preserve meaning.

### 10.2 Digital

Deliver the logo as optimized SVG for web and native vector/PDF/EPS for print workflows. Use PNG only where an application cannot render a vector, and export at the exact required display density; never use a screenshot as a logo source. Set color in sRGB for web and test the Paper/Ink relationship on common displays. Supply `@font-face` files only under valid font distribution terms and use robust fallback stacks.

Raster photography should be appropriately licensed, color-managed, and compressed to its display need without visible artifacts. Provide meaningful alternative text for informative images; decorative images should have empty alt text. Never embed essential text in an image. Export documents to tagged PDF when accessibility is required, with logical reading order, selectable text, bookmarks for long reports, and sufficient contrast.

### 10.3 Asset handoff

A production handoff should contain the approved logo masters, color tokens, type files or approved sourcing instructions, icon source files, component library, and this specification. Name assets predictably, for example: `CC_Logo_Horizontal_Terra-Paper.svg`, `CC_Mark_Ink.svg`, and `CC_Mark_Paper-on-Ink.svg`. Keep a small change log. Any new lockup, sub-brand, color, or mark alteration requires brand-owner review before release.

## 11. Favicon, app icon, and social avatar

The **emblem-only squared spiral/C** is the only mark appropriate for a favicon or social avatar. Never attempt to fit the wordmark into these small canvases.

For a favicon, use a square Terra field with the Paper emblem centered and optically scaled to approximately 68–72% of the canvas. Export a tested SVG plus 16, 32, and 48 px PNG/ICO variants as required by the product environment. At 16 px, simplify only through the approved small-size master; do not manually erase structural features. The mark must remain readable against browser dark and light chrome.

For a social avatar, begin from a 1:1 **1024 × 1024 px** master canvas. Center the approved emblem within a **14% safe area** on all sides. The preferred avatar is Terra with a Paper mark; the alternate is Ink with a Paper mark and a controlled Terra emblem treatment from the approved master. Keep the field flat, without wordmark, tagline, gradients, scenery, small labels, or decorative border. Test the avatar in circular and rounded-square crops before publishing.

## 12. System-wide do / do not rules

| Do | Do not |
|---|---|
| Lead with a specific operating outcome and a clear next step. | Lead with vague AI claims, speculative promises, or technological spectacle. |
| Use Paper, Ink, type hierarchy, and rules to create order. | Fill every surface with cards, shadows, borders, or color. |
| Treat Terra as primary action and the other inks as semantic states. | Use all accent colors as decoration or a default categorical rainbow. |
| Pair real business imagery with clear, documentary context. | Use neon AI clichés, robots, generic blue tech, or unverified synthetic faces. |
| Give diagrams labels, inputs, owners, outputs, and a reading direction. | Use unlabeled arrows, ambiguous loops, 3D effects, or color-only meaning. |
| Use Libertinus for editorial authority and Inter for practical clarity. | Use display serif for dense UI, paragraph all-caps, or decorative replacement fonts. |
| Preserve the approved mark and its clear space. | Recreate, distort, crop, outline, animate, pattern, or claim legal status for the mark. |
| Test contrast, focus, scale, and grayscale interpretation. | Treat a nominal hex value as proof that a rendered experience is accessible. |

## 13. Approval checklist
