# Orange Ocean → Cypress Command: Controlled 30-Day Rebrand and Migration Plan

**Purpose.** This plan moves the business identity from **Orange Ocean** to **Cypress Command** through a calm, technically controlled transition. It is designed to preserve client continuity, protect discoverability, and minimize unnecessary attention to the former brand. The public story is limited to a brief forward-looking update. It does not discuss, investigate, or repeat any historical matters associated with the former name.

> **Operational guidance, not legal advice.** A qualified attorney and tax/accounting advisers should confirm entity-name availability, trademark strategy, contract amendments, tax registrations, and any required notices before the business relies on this plan.

## Executive decisions to make before Day 1

| Decision | Recommended position | Decision owner | Evidence of completion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Public framing | Use a simple forward-looking identity update; do not explain or defend the change. | Principal | Approved message pack |
| Public use of “Orange Ocean” | Mention it only as a minimal continuity label for **60 days after launch**: in the site notice, redirect banner, email signature transition line, and direct communications. Do not use it in posts, campaigns, press releases, or new marketing pages. | Principal + communications lead | Approved exposure window |
| Legal entity name | Do not assume the brand name and legal entity name must change together. Confirm the selected entity approach before filing or presenting the brand as a legal name. | Principal + counsel | Written legal decision |
| Launch date | Launch on a low-volume weekday morning after DNS, mail, redirects, and rollback checks pass. | Project lead | Signed launch go/no-go |
| Legacy-domain retention | Retain and renew the former primary domain for at least **24 months**; keep HTTPS active and redirect it permanently. Retain critical typo/alternate domains if budget permits. | IT/domain owner | Renewal and redirect records |

## Communication guardrails

The transition should be **calm, sparse, and consistent**. Announce once through owned channels and individually to active clients, key prospects, vendors, and partners. Do not create a public FAQ, long-form explanation, or media pitch. Do not comment on motives beyond the approved response below. Do not answer speculative questions, share personal context, or debate the prior brand.

**Recommended public mention period:** Use “formerly Orange Ocean” only for 60 days after the new-site launch. On Day 61, remove it from public pages, bio lines, signatures, banners, metadata, and routine correspondence. Keep the former name only where operationally required, including legal filings, historical invoices, archival records, redirects, and an internal support script. If a contractual or regulatory requirement calls for a longer reference, meet that requirement narrowly and without promotional placement.

**Message approval rule:** Only the principal or delegated communications lead may approve deviations from the text below. Every customer-facing team member should copy the approved response rather than improvise.

### Website transition notice — exact copy

> **Cypress Command is the new name for Orange Ocean.** Our services and team remain available as usual. Please update your bookmarks to [new-domain].

**Placement and removal:** Place this notice on the former-domain landing page and as a small, dismissible banner on the new homepage only. Do not place it in the homepage hero, navigation, title tag, primary metadata, or new service pages. Remove the new-site banner after 60 days. The former-domain landing page may retain the concise notice only if a full-site redirect is not technically possible; otherwise use a direct 301 redirect without a public page.

### Social bio line — exact copy

> Cypress Command — formerly Orange Ocean. Same team, forward focus. [new-domain]

Use this line for 60 days, then replace it with:

> Cypress Command. [Short value proposition.] [new-domain]

### Response if asked why the name changed — exact copy

> We chose Cypress Command as a forward-looking brand that better reflects where we are going. The team and our commitment to clients have not changed. We are focused on the work ahead.

If pressed, repeat the first sentence once, then redirect: “I don’t have anything further to add on the naming decision, but I’m happy to help with [service/project/account question].” Do not say “no comment,” speculate, correct rumors, or compare the two names.

## Technical migration checklist

### Redirects, search, analytics, and backlinks

Use **server-side 301 redirects** for permanent page moves. Redirect each old URL to the closest relevant new page, not automatically to the new homepage. Preserve path and useful query parameters where possible. Avoid meta-refresh redirects, JavaScript-only redirects, redirect chains, loops, and bulk redirects that send unrelated content to the homepage. Leave redirects running for at least 24 months, and longer for URLs with durable backlinks, printed materials, or ongoing direct traffic.

Before launch, export the legacy sitemap, web analytics landing-page report, Search Console indexed-pages and links reports, CRM campaign URLs, paid-ad destinations, and known printed or QR-code URLs. Use them to populate the map. After launch, submit the new sitemap, maintain verified properties for both domains, and use the available Search Console site-move tools only after the redirects are live and tested. Retain legacy analytics to compare the change; annotate the launch date in reporting.

Track daily during the first two weeks: organic sessions, branded and nonbranded search impressions, indexed new URLs, crawl errors, 404s by referrer, redirect response times, form conversion rate, email delivery/bounce/spam rates, direct traffic to legacy URLs, and client-service issues. Review weekly through Day 90. Escalate a sustained material decline in leads, traffic, indexing, or delivery to the project lead and web/IT owners.

### Email and identity controls

Create role-based new addresses (for example, `hello@`, `support@`, `billing@`) and map each old address to an owned destination. Publish SPF and DKIM before switching outbound mail. Begin DMARC with monitoring (`p=none`) and move to a stricter policy only after legitimate senders are identified and aligned. Update email signatures on a scheduled date and use the transition phrase for 60 days only. Check all third-party senders, including CRM, invoicing, customer support, e-signature, calendar, forms, and marketing platforms; these often fail authentication after a domain change.

### Contracts, invoices, and records

For all documents, distinguish clearly between a **public brand** and the **legal contracting entity**. Existing agreements remain governed by their terms; do not state that contracts automatically change without counsel confirmation. Where a counterparty needs an update, use counsel-approved notice or amendment language and track acknowledgment. Preserve historical contracts, invoices, receipts, tax records, email records, and branding assets under the normal retention policy. Update only future-issued templates after approval, retaining version/date control.

### Directories and profiles

Start with records that affect client contact, payments, search, or verification: Google Business Profile, maps, LinkedIn company page, major social accounts, client portals, payment processors, bank/insurance profiles, trade associations, licensing bodies, marketplaces, review sites, email sender profiles, and partner directory pages. For each entry, record its URL, login owner, current name, new name, submitted date, expected propagation date, proof, and whether the old listing must remain as a redirect/alias. Prevent duplicates, which can confuse customers and fragment reviews or search visibility.

## Redirect map template

Maintain this in a controlled spreadsheet and export it to a version-controlled CSV used by the web team. `Old URL` must be the exact requested path. `Target URL` must be the closest content match. Use **301** unless counsel or the web lead documents an exception.

| Priority | Old URL | Target URL | Status | Query-string rule | Owner | Test date | Test result | Notes |
|---|---|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | `https://orangeocean.example/` | `https://cypresscommand.example/` | 301 | Preserve where relevant | Web lead | YYYY-MM-DD | Pass/Fail | Homepage |
| P0 | `https://orangeocean.example/contact` | `https://cypresscommand.example/contact` | 301 | Preserve campaign parameters | Web lead | YYYY-MM-DD | Pass/Fail | Form test required |
| P1 | `https://orangeocean.example/services/[service]` | `https://cypresscommand.example/services/[service]` | 301 | Preserve where relevant | Web lead | YYYY-MM-DD | Pass/Fail | Closest equivalent |
| P1 | `https://orangeocean.example/resources/[file]` | `https://cypresscommand.example/resources/[file]` | 301 | Preserve where relevant | Web lead | YYYY-MM-DD | Pass/Fail | File/download destination |
| P2 | `https://orangeocean.example/[retired-page]` | `https://cypresscommand.example/[closest-relevant-page]` | 301 | Document exceptions | Web lead | YYYY-MM-DD | Pass/Fail | Never default blindly to homepage |

**CSV header:**

```csv
priority,old_url,target_url,http_status,query_string_rule,owner,test_date,test_result,notes
```

**Acceptance tests:** Each mapped legacy URL returns exactly one 301 hop to a live 200-page destination; the destination has the correct canonical URL; important query parameters are preserved; form and conversion paths work; no redirect points to staging; and no destination carries a temporary `noindex` directive.

## Risks, early-warning signs, and rollback points

| Risk | Early-warning sign | Prevention | Rollback point / action | Decision owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Name conflict or filing problem | Counsel identifies a conflict, opposition, or unavailable filing | Complete legal/entity and trademark review before public use | Pause public launch; retain prior legal/brand-facing materials until counsel clears an alternative | Executive sponsor + counsel |
| Domain/email compromise or lockout | Unexpected registrar changes, lost access, failed MFA | Registrar lock, shared secure ownership, MFA, recovery records | Revert DNS only through two-person approval; contact registrar security immediately | IT lead |
| DNS, TLS, or website outage | New domain does not resolve, certificate errors, material 5xx rate | Stage and rehearse; maintain old hosting; lower TTL only as planned | Restore last known-good DNS/hosting config; display a neutral maintenance page if approved | IT lead |
| Broken redirects or loss of search traffic | 404 spike, crawl errors, traffic/lead decline, redirects to homepage | URL inventory, one-to-one map, prelaunch crawl, long-term legacy domain | Restore prior redirect rules or apply targeted fixes; do not remove old domain | Web lead |
| Email interruption or spoofing | Bounces, spam placement, failed authentication, client reports | Dual delivery tests, SPF/DKIM/DMARC monitoring, legacy forwarding | Revert primary outbound sender while retaining new site; restore prior routing rule from export | IT lead |
| Client/vendor payment or contract confusion | Payment rejections, vendor requests for name mismatch, procurement holds | Direct notice; distinguish brand from legal entity; counsel-approved templates | Continue using existing legal payee/document wording until counterparties are updated | Finance lead + counsel |
| Inconsistent public information | Duplicate profiles, stale bios, mixed logos, incorrect contact data | Inventory and owner tracker; priority order | Restore a verified listing or hide/claim duplicate; correct highest-traffic records first | Communications lead |
| Unwanted attention or speculative inquiry | Repeated questions, online comments, media/contact requests | Sparse message, trained spokespeople, no extended explanation | Pause scheduled posts and route all inquiries to one spokesperson using the approved response | Executive sponsor + communications lead |
| Irreversible asset retirement | Deleted inbox, cancelled domain, lost account history | 90-day minimum hold; backup and retention inventory | Do not retire; restore from backup or re-enable service before termination window ends | Project lead + IT lead |

**Mandatory stop-the-line conditions.** Pause the launch or revert the affected component if the name is not cleared for intended use; the production site cannot serve HTTPS reliably; any P0 redirect fails; client email cannot send and receive reliably; payment or client portal access is impaired; a security incident occurs; or the executive sponsor withdraws approval. A component rollback does not require abandoning the overall rebrand: keep messages sparse, state only that a technical update is being completed if a customer needs an explanation, and relaunch after the specific gate passes.

## 30-, 60-, and 90-day follow-through

At **Day 30**, close the launch project only after assigning remaining directory claims, contract updates, and technical defects. At **Day 60**, remove the public “formerly Orange Ocean” continuity language from the new website, bios, signatures, and standard templates; retain it only in technical redirects and necessary historical/legal records. At **Day 90**, assess indexing, referral traffic, direct legacy-domain traffic, email volumes to old addresses, client issues, and vendor-account completion. Keep redirects and legacy domain control in place for the full recommended retention period.
